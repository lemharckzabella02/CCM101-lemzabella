# GCP Research

## Brief Overview
Google Cloud Platform launched in 2008 and runs on the same backbone infrastructure Google uses for Search and YouTube. It's the youngest of the big three in terms of enterprise adoption, but it's carved out a strong reputation in data analytics and AI/ML.

## Global Infrastructure
GCP is built around Regions and Zones, with around 40 regions worldwide. One thing that makes it stand out is Google's private global fiber network connecting its data centers, which keeps latency low between regions.

## Cloud Management Console
Google Cloud Console

## Four Core Services
1. Compute Engine — virtual machines
2. Cloud Storage — object storage
3. Cloud SQL — managed relational database
4. IAM (Identity and Access Management) — access control

## Three Advantages
1. **Best-in-class AI and data analytics tools** — services like BigQuery and Vertex AI are widely considered the strongest in the industry, since Google builds a lot of its own products on the same stack.
2. **Kubernetes origin** — Google created Kubernetes and open-sourced it, so GKE (Google Kubernetes Engine) is often seen as the most mature managed Kubernetes offering.
3. **Competitive, simple pricing** — GCP is generally seen as having more straightforward, often cheaper pricing for sustained workloads compared to AWS and Azure.

## Typical Enterprise Use Cases
GCP is the go-to choice for companies doing heavy data analysis, machine learning, or running containerized workloads at scale — think an AI research company training models that need high-performance computing. It's less common as a "default" enterprise pick compared to AWS or Azure, but it wins out specifically when AI/ML or Kubernetes are the priority.
