# AWS Research

## Brief Overview
Launched in 2006 by Amazon, AWS is the oldest and currently the largest cloud provider by market share, offering over 200 services covering compute, storage, databases, networking, AI/ML, and more.

## Global Infrastructure
Organized into Regions (geographic areas) and Availability Zones (isolated data centers within a region). As of 2026, AWS has 30+ regions worldwide, plus Edge Locations for content delivery via CloudFront.

## Cloud Management Console
AWS Management Console

## Four Core Services
1. EC2 (Elastic Compute Cloud) — virtual machines/compute
2. S3 (Simple Storage Service) — object storage
3. RDS (Relational Database Service) — managed SQL databases
4. IAM (Identity and Access Management) — access control

## Three Advantages
1. **Market maturity and breadth of services** — AWS has the largest service catalog of any provider, plus the most third-party tooling and community support due to being first to market.
2. **Global infrastructure scale** — the largest number of Availability Zones and Edge Locations, giving low-latency access in most regions worldwide.
3. **Strong security and compliance** — widely used by government, financial, and healthcare sectors due to its extensive compliance certifications (HIPAA, FedRAMP, etc.).

## Typical Enterprise Use Cases
AWS is commonly used by startups needing to scale quickly without upfront infrastructure investment, media/streaming companies needing content delivery at scale, and enterprises running large-scale data processing or machine learning workloads. Its maturity also makes it a default choice for companies without existing ties to Microsoft or Google ecosystems.
